## Preface: The One Strategic Truth

Your COGS on a fragrance product is genuinely trivial — pence per unit. Your real constraint is not margin; it is **CAC against a £26 AOV**. Every decision in this plan is designed to keep CAC near zero for the first 60 days, because £160 of Meta spend will not buy you a profitable business. It buys you **data**. Organic content, seeding, and subscriber retention are the actual levers. Paid media amplifies what already works; it does not create it.

**The second truth, added in v2.0:** your product's commercial risk is not that it smells wrong on day one. It is that it looks cheap or smells dead on day thirty. Wood staining, cold-weather clouding, leakage and scent fractionation are what generate refunds and one-star reviews in this category. Module 2C exists because those failures are invisible until you deliberately test for them.

## Module 2: Sourcing, Assembly & UK CLP Compliance

### 2.1 The Component Assembly Model

You are not manufacturing. You are combining three commodity inputs — hardware, fragrance oil, carrier base — under a brand.

#### Hardware — Import from China

- **Platforms:** Alibaba, 1688 (via sourcing agent), Made-in-China
- **Search terms that return the right SKUs:** `8ml wooden cap glass hanging car diffuser bottle`, `beech wood cap essential oil bottle with rope`, `car vent clip aluminium refillable aroma`, `matte black car perfume clip empty`
- **Keep each order under £135.** Below that threshold customs duty relief applies. You still pay import VAT but avoid duty and large courier handling fees. Split larger orders.
- **Always buy a paid sample set first** (£15–25 incl. shipping, often credited against bulk). Check: does the wooden cap actually wick, or is it lacquered shut? Is the stopper leak-tight when inverted 24 hours? Is the black finish a durable powder coat or sprayed?

Do not import fragrance oil. You need a UK-issued SDS for compliance, and hazard classification risk on imported material is not worth it at this scale.

#### The Flashpoint Rule

Royal Mail prohibits flammable liquids in the post. **Only buy oils with a closed-cup flashpoint above 60.5°C, ideally above 70°C.** Check SDS Section 9.1, or the Tech Specs / Properties table on Candle Shack and Supplies for Candles. Woody, amber, oud and leather oils typically run 81–100°C+, so this filter rarely costs you anything in the families you care about. It does bite on citrus-forward and some fresh oils.

#### Blend Ratio — CORRECTED

**Formulate at 20–25% fragrance oil in Augeo Clean Multi.** Candle Shack's product pages carry an explicit "Suitable for Diffusers" figure, and on the oils checked it reads **25%**.

The original 60% plan was wrong, and materially so:

- **It breaches the supplier's stated IFRA limit,** which undermines the IFRA certificate you are relying on for compliance
- **It raises classification risk** — more concentrated mixture, more likely to trigger H315/H317/H319 and possibly H304, which is exactly what forces a child-resistant closure and tactile warning triangle
- **It degrades solubility.** Augeo is your solubiliser. At 60% oil there is too little of it to hold non-polar sesquiterpenes and resinoids in solution, and you get haze at low temperature
- **It increases wood-clogging risk** by pushing more heavy material through the same capillary path

**Solve intensity through oil selection and hardware, not fragrance load.** A well-chosen 25% blend with a woody-amber backbone out-projects a 60% blend of a badly-chosen citrus-topped oil, lasts just as long, and costs less per unit.

#### Assembly Protocol

Weigh in grams, never measure by volume. Blend in a glass beaker, **mature 48–72 hours** before bottling (the profile changes materially), bottle, then rest 24 hours before photographing or shipping. **Log every batch:** batch number, oil lot, ratio, date. You need this if you ever face a complaint or Trading Standards enquiry.

#### Augeo Handling Warning (new in v2.0)

**Augeo Clean Multi strips paint and polish on contact.** For an automotive product this is a genuine handling and labelling consideration:

- Include a clear "avoid contact with painted, polished and plastic surfaces — wipe spills immediately" line on the insert card and product page FAQ
- Design refill packaging to minimise drip risk (pipette or narrow-neck flat bottle, not a wide-mouth vial)
- Add it to your leak-test acceptance criteria in Module 2C

### 2.2 UK CLP Compliance

Your finished product is a **chemical mixture** under GB CLP. Selling it without a compliant label is a criminal offence, and Trading Standards does enforce against small home-fragrance businesses.

#### Step 1 — Get the paperwork free

Every reputable UK supplier provides, per oil, at no cost: **SDS**, **IFRA certificate**, **allergen declaration**, and **CLP data at common usage percentages**. Candle Shack's free CLP Label Design Tool and ScentFusion will output a compliant label from your formulation. Supplies for Candles has an equivalent generator. That is your entire compliance toolchain at £0.

#### Step 2 — Mandatory label elements

- Trading name, full UK address, contact telephone number
- Product identifier (trade name plus oil name)
- Nominal quantity (8ml / 10ml)
- Hazard pictogram(s) — typically GHS07 and/or GHS09
- Signal word: **"Warning"** or **"Danger"**
- All applicable **H statements** (e.g. H315, H317, H319, H411)
- All applicable **P statements** (e.g. P101, P102, P273, P280, P302+P352, P305+P351+P338)
- **EUH208** where a sensitiser sits below the classification threshold
- **EUH210** — "Safety data sheet available on request"
- "Keep out of reach of children"

#### Step 3 — Solving the small-package label problem

For packages up to 3 litres, CLP specifies a minimum label of 52 × 74mm with pictograms at least 10 × 10mm. An 8ml bottle cannot carry that. CLP permits **fold-out labels, tie-on tags, or the outer packaging** where shape makes a standard label impossible.

**Your solution:** a black tie-on swing tag on the diffuser cord carrying the full CLP block, repeated on the base of the outer box. Compliant, and in matte black with white type it reads as a luxury hangtag rather than a warning.

#### Step 4 — UFI and Poison Centre Notification

GB CLP (England, Scotland, Wales) is administered by HSE. A **UFI is not mandatory** for GB-only sales. UFI plus PCN via the ECHA portal are mandatory for Northern Ireland and EU markets.

**Geo-restrict Shopify shipping to England, Scotland and Wales for the first 60 days.** This removes the UFI/PCN obligation, the EU GPSR Responsible Person requirement, and all customs paperwork, at a cost of roughly 4% of addressable market.

> From 21 May 2026, the requirement to notify HSE of classification and labelling of *substances* placed on the GB market was removed. Older guidance telling you to file a GB CLP substance notification is outdated.

#### Step 5 — Child-resistant fastenings and tactile warnings

Check SDS Section 2.2. If your finished mixture classifies for aspiration hazard (**H304**), skin corrosion, certain STOT categories, or acute toxicity, CLP Annex II requires a **child-resistant closure** and a **raised tactile warning triangle**.

**The driver is terpene content.** H304 classification follows from low kinematic viscosity plus hydrocarbon content — d-limonene, α-pinene and similar. Citrus-forward and high-terpene oils are the usual trigger. **Screen SDS Section 3 for total d-limonene; treat anything above 10% as a red flag** and either reject the oil or reduce its percentage until the mixture falls below threshold.

Note also that **coumarin** (tonka) and **linalool**, **limonene**, **citral**, **eugenol**, **isoeugenol** are declarable allergens. These do not trigger a CRC but will appear on your label via EUH208 or H317.

#### Step 6 — What not to do

- Never make skin, therapeutic or "aromatherapy benefit" claims. That shifts you into cosmetic or medicinal regulation.
- Keep a batch log and SDS folder from Day 1.
- Product liability insurance is non-negotiable for a chemical mixture used inside a vehicle.

## Module 2B: Scent Architecture & Discovery Shortlist

### 2B.1 The chemist's brief — what governs performance in a car

A hanging wooden-cap diffuser is not a small reed diffuser. Three physical facts should drive every selection decision.

**Wicking through beech is a molecular-weight filter.** Capillary draw through untreated hardwood *fractionates* your blend. Low-MW, high-vapour-pressure molecules (limonene, linalool, aldehydes C10–C12, dihydromyrcenol, Calone) migrate and flash off first. High-MW molecules (Galaxolide MW 258, Iso E Super MW 234, Ambroxan MW 236, patchouli sesquiterpenes, resinoids) migrate slowly and, in the case of large polycyclic musks and natural resins, deposit in the wood grain. Within 10–14 days an unbalanced oil will have burned off its top and heart entirely, leaving a flat woody smell and a cap beginning to glaze. **This is the number one cause of "it stopped smelling after two weeks" reviews in this category.**

**The "cabin-fill hit" and "resistance to airflow" are two different problems.** The entry hit is *headspace saturation*: the car sits closed, interior surfaces reach 40–70°C in summer, vapour accumulates in roughly 2.5m³ with nowhere to escape. Almost any oil delivers this. Resistance to airflow is *emission rate under forced convection*: with HVAC running at 3–4 air changes per minute, perceived intensity depends on how fast the wood surface re-supplies vapour. That favours **mid-volatility, high-substantivity molecules** — Iso E Super, Ambroxan, Cashmeran, Habanolide, Javanol, Clearwood — rather than either ultralight ozonics or ultraheavy resins.

**The molecules you want sit in a narrow band.** Target oils whose character is carried by woody-ambers and musks **in the heart as well as the base**. An oil with a musk-only base and a citrus-only top is the worst possible construction for this format.

### 2B.2 The three screening gates

| Gate | Test | Where to find it | Reject if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Flashpoint** | Closed-cup FP | Candle Shack "Tech Specs"; SFC "Properties"; others SDS §9.1 | FP ≤ 60.5°C |
| **2. CRC trigger** | H304, Skin Corr., STOT | SDS §2.2; check §3 for d-limonene / α-pinene | H304 present, or d-limonene >10% |
| **3. Solubility & wood** | Haze and staining | **Physical test only — no supplier publishes this** | Cloud point above 0°C, or beech browning inside 30 days |

**On gate 3: vanillin and ethyl vanillin are your discolouration enemies.** They oxidise brown, stain pale beech, and crystallise out of solution in cold weather. Any heavily gourmand oil needs the 30-day wood test before commitment. This is the most common reason gourmand car diffusers look cheap after a month.

#### Family 2 — Soapy / laundry clean

**Be honest about this family.** It is the hardest for your format. A clean-linen 8ml vessel smells magnificent for eight days and thins by day 20.

Two solutions:
1. Choose only clean accords with a **musk-amber base**, never pure aldehyde-ozone builds
2. **Sell this family as the vent clip SKU**, where the pad is cheap, refreshed often, and longevity expectation is lower. This is the correct commercial answer.

## Module 2C: Physical Testing Protocol

This module is the difference between a brand and a returns queue. Every test below exists because a specific, documented failure mode destroys either the product or the customer's trust in it.

### 2C.0 Test build specification

Blend **all candidates identically** so the only variable is the oil.

- **Ratio:** 25% fragrance oil, 75% Augeo Clean Multi, **by weight**
- **Batch size:** 20g per candidate (enough for one 8ml vessel, one cold-test vial, one control vial, plus spare)
- **Maturation:** seal and rest **72 hours at room temperature, in the dark**, before any assessment
- **Vessels:** identical 8ml beechwood-cap bottles from the same supplier batch
- **Controls:** one vessel filled with **pure Augeo, no fragrance** (isolates carrier effects on wood), and one vial of **each neat oil** stored dark (reference for colour drift)
- **Labelling:** code each vessel with a number only. Do not label by scent name. You will unconsciously score a scent you like more generously.

Record a **day-0 baseline** for every unit: gross weight to 0.01g, photograph of cap and bottle under fixed lighting, and a written description of the neat blend's smell.

### 2C.1 The six test environments

| # | Environment | Setup | Duration | Primarily tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **Ambient bench control** | Indoor shelf, 18–22°C, out of direct light | 42 days | Baseline evaporation curve, colour drift, wood staining without thermal stress |
| **E2** | **Cold soak** | Domestic fridge at 4°C for 24h, then freezer at −5°C for 4h, then return to room temp | Repeat weekly | **Cloud point / solubility.** UK winter cabins routinely sit below 0°C overnight |
| **E3** | **Heat soak** | Sealed car, south-facing, parked in sun. Log peak with a min-max thermometer on the dashboard | Every dry day, 42 days | Thermal expansion, leakage, accelerated oxidation, headspace saturation strength |
| **E4** | **Dynamic airflow** | Same car, engine running, HVAC on setting 2, vents at mid-dash, 60-second exposure | Weekly | **Emission rate under forced convection.** The test nobody runs. |
| **E5** | **UV exposure** | Windowsill in direct daylight, unshaded | 42 days | Photodegradation, oil colour shift, label and cord fade |
| **E6** | **Vibration / transit** | Sealed in a shipping mailer, taped inside a vehicle boot during normal driving | 14 days | Leak integrity, cap loosening, closure failure under real transit conditions |

**Minimum viable setup:** if you can only run three, run **E2 (cold), E3 (heat) and E4 (airflow).** Those three catch the failures that generate refunds.

### 2C.2 What you are physically looking for

This is the checklist to have in hand at every inspection.

#### In the liquid

| Observation | What it means | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Haze or cloudiness that clears on warming** | Cloud point above test temperature. Oil is at the edge of solubility | Moderate — reduce oil % or reject |
| **Haze that does NOT clear on warming** | Permanent incompatibility with Augeo | **Reject** |
| **Two distinct layers / separation** | Solubility failure | **Reject** |
| **Crystals or solid deposits** | Vanillin, coumarin or resin crystallisation out of solution in cold | **Reject** for this format |
| **Colour deepening from pale to amber/brown** | Oxidation. Accelerates in heat and UV | Moderate — acceptable if slow and the glass is tinted; fatal in clear glass |
| **Sediment at the bottom** | Resinoid or natural material dropping out | Moderate to reject |
| **Viscosity increase / stickiness** | Polymerisation or solvent loss. Will clog the cap | **Reject** |

#### At the wood cap

| Observation | What it means | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Browning of pale beech** | Vanillin / ethyl vanillin staining, or oxidation | **Primary reject criterion.** Visible browning by day 30 = fail |
| **Dark tidemark or oil line at the wood/glass junction** | Capillary wicking leaving a residue ring | Moderate — looks cheap, photographs badly |
| **Glazing or shiny film on the wood surface** | Heavy musk / resin deposition blocking the capillary path | **Reject** — this is what kills throw at week three |
| **Swelling or splitting of the cap** | Solvent attack on the wood, or humidity cycling | **Reject** |
| **Cap loosening in the neck** | Thermal cycling or wood dimensional change. Leak risk | **Reject** |
| **Wood odour breakthrough** (raw timber smell) | Poor-quality or insufficiently seasoned beech | Hardware problem, not oil — change supplier |

#### At the bottle, closure and cord

| Observation | What it means | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Any oil on the outside of the glass** | Leak or seepage | **Reject** — this is an automotive product; Augeo strips paint and polish |
| **Oil wicking up the cord** | Capillary travel along fibre. Will drip onto upholstery | **Reject** or redesign the cord attachment |
| **Cord discolouration or stiffening** | Oil migration | Moderate |
| **Label lifting, curling or ink bleed** | Wrong label stock — you need waterproof matte vinyl, not paper | Fix with materials, not oil choice |
| **Condensation inside the glass** | Humidity ingress through the cap | Moderate — expected to a degree, monitor |
| **Glass crazing or stress marks after heat soak** | Poor annealing in the hardware | **Reject the hardware supplier** |

#### At the nose

| Observation | What it means | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Character drift — the scent no longer smells like day 0** | **Fractionation.** Top and heart have wicked off, leaving only the base | The core failure mode. Score weekly |
| **Sour, waxy or "off" note developing** | Oxidation, usually of terpenes or aldehydes | **Reject** |
| **Plastic or solvent note** | Augeo becoming perceptible as fragrance depletes | Late-stage, expected — note the week it appears |
| **Strong static hit but nothing under airflow** | Insufficient mid-volatility material. The "vanishes when the AC is on" complaint | **Reject for the vessel SKU** — may still work as a vent clip |

### 2C.3 Scoring sheet

Score every unit weekly on this five-point scale. Keep it in a single spreadsheet, one row per unit per week.

| Metric | Scale | Fail threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Static throw** (E3, door opened after 8h closed) | 1 = undetectable · 3 = clearly present · 5 = fills the cabin | Below 3 at any point in weeks 1–4 |
| **Dynamic throw** (E4, 60s with HVAC on 2) | 1 = gone · 3 = clearly present · 5 = undiminished | Below 2, **or a drop of more than 2 points from static** |
| **Character fidelity** | 1 = unrecognisable vs day 0 · 5 = identical | Below 3 by week 4 |
| **Liquid clarity** (E2, assessed cold) | 1 = separated · 3 = slight haze · 5 = water-clear | Below 4 |
| **Cap appearance** | 1 = heavily stained/glazed · 5 = as new | Below 4 at day 30 |
| **Mass loss** | Weigh to 0.01g | **More than 40% loss by week 3** predicts failure before week 6 |

**A candidate passes only if it clears every threshold.** Do not promote an oil because it smells wonderful and fails one physical test. The physical failures are the ones customers photograph.

### 2C.4 The 42-day schedule

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **0** | Blend all candidates at 25%. Seal, label with codes only, rest in the dark |
| **3** | Maturation complete. Record baseline: weight, photographs, written scent description. Assemble vessels. Distribute to E1–E6 |
| **4** | First E2 cold cycle. **Cloud point failures usually appear here** — this is your earliest and cheapest reject gate |
| **7** | Week 1 scoring across all metrics. Second cold cycle. First E4 airflow test |
| **10** | E6 transit units removed and inspected for leaks |
| **14** | **Checkpoint 1.** Week 2 scoring. By now fractionation is visible in weak candidates. Cut anything failing dynamic throw or clarity. Order hardware in bulk for survivors |
| **21** | Week 3 scoring. **Mass-loss gate** — anything above 40% loss is cut |
| **30** | **Checkpoint 2 — the wood-staining verdict.** Photograph every cap against the day-0 image under identical lighting. This is the decisive gate. **Place the £95 bulk oil order for survivors only.** |
| **35** | Week 5 scoring. Character fidelity is the key metric now |
| **42** | **Close-out.** Final scoring. Confirm 4–6 week longevity claim is defensible. Lock the launch range. Write the honest intensity gauge and longevity figures for each product page from this data, not from marketing instinct |

### 2C.5 Turning test data into product page copy

The output of this protocol is not just a go/no-go. It is the raw material for the honest product page described in Module 3.

- **Intensity gauge fill** comes from your week-1 static throw score, not from how much you like the scent
- **Longevity claim** comes from the mass-loss curve, stated conservatively. If a unit hits 40% loss at week 4, claim "4–5 weeks," not "up to 8"
- **"Reads as" line** comes from your week-4 character description, because that is what the customer lives with, not the day-1 opening
- **FAQ answers** on leaking, summer heat and staining come directly from E3 and E6 results. You will be able to answer them with evidence instead of hope
- **The day-3 usage email** (Module 6, flow 4) should reference the actual peak-throw day you observed, which is typically day 2–4 as the wood saturates

### 2C.6 Expected outcomes

Based on the molecular architecture of the shortlist, the likely results:

- **Family 6 (night) and Family 3 (skin) should pass comfortably.** High-MW, high-flashpoint, low terpene, minimal vanillin.
- **Family 4 (gourmand) will pass on throw and may fail on wood staining.** Watch Dark Honey & Tobacco and any Black Opium type closely at day 30.
- **Family 1 (fruit) is the coin flip.** Peach Couture's labdanum/leather base should carry it; a lighter fruit build will fractionate.
- **Families 2 and 5 (laundry, aquatic) will most likely fail dynamic throw in the 8ml vessel.** Plan now to route them to the vent clip SKU rather than discover it on a returns spreadsheet.

**If I had to bet on four survivors:** Cashmeran, Sahara, Peach Couture, and Dark Honey & Tobacco. Three of those are carried by Cashmeran-family, saffron-leather, and tonka-amber molecules respectively — all squarely in the mid-volatility, high-substantivity band.

> Hope the [scent] landed well. If you end up filming it, I'd love to set you up with a personal code — 20% off for your audience and 20% of every order back to you, paid monthly. No pressure either way.

**Affiliate structure:** 20% commission, 20% customer discount. On a £39 bundle that's £7.80 to the creator, leaving ~£19 contribution. Track with native discount codes and a spreadsheet for the first 90 days. Escalate to 25% plus free monthly refills for anyone driving 10+ orders.

### 5.2 Automating discovery and outreach

- **Discovery:** native hashtag search (#cardetailing, #carinterior, #quietluxury, #ukcars), then the "suggested accounts" sidebar — the best free discovery graph that exists. Mine comment sections of large detailing accounts.
- **Scraping:** Apify free tier, Instagram Profile Scraper and TikTok Scraper actors. Export to CSV with followers, bio, recent engagement.
- **Filtering:** *"Here are 400 scraped profiles. Filter to UK-based accounts with 1,000–15,000 followers whose bio or recent captions indicate car ownership, detailing or automotive lifestyle content. Exclude dealerships, brands and agencies. Rank by estimated engagement rate; output a table with handle, followers, engagement rate, and a one-line reason they fit."*
- **Personalisation:** *"For each of these 40 creators, write a one-sentence opening referencing something specific and verifiable from their most recent caption (column D). No compliments about their 'amazing content'. Be specific or say nothing."*
- **Send manually.** Automated DM tools get accounts restricted. 25–40 creators is about two hours.

### 5.3 The £90 paid test framework

**Do not spend before Day 35.** Its only purpose is amplifying a creative that has already proven itself organically.

**Prerequisite:** one organic video above 20,000 views, or a seeded creator post above 4% engagement.

**Meta (£50):** one campaign, Sales objective; one ad set, broad UK 25–50, all placements, Advantage+ audience on. No manual interest stacks — at this budget they starve the algorithm. £10/day for 5 days. 4 creatives, each a different hook, identical offer and landing page. **Kill at day 3** anything below 0.8% CTR or above £1.20 CPC.

**TikTok Spark Ads (£40):** amplify a creator's organic post from their own handle, preserving its existing engagement. Request the Spark Ad authorisation code. £10/day for 4 days. Usually the higher-ROAS half for this category.

**What you're buying:** not sales — the answer to "which hook works," which you then apply to 100+ organic pieces at zero cost. Judge on hook rate and cost per add-to-cart, not immediate ROAS. At a £26 AOV on cold traffic, ROAS of 0.8–1.5 is normal and is not failure.

## Module 6: Post-Purchase Retention & Data Analysis

### 6.1 Email automation architecture

All flows in native Shopify Email.

| # | Flow | Trigger | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Abandoned checkout (3-part)** | Checkout started, not completed | +1h, +22h, +72h | 1: no discount, repeat the scent pyramid and "reads as" line. 2: address the objection ("Worried you won't like the scent? Every order ships with a 30-day scent guarantee"). 3: 10% code, 24h expiry |
| 2 | **Browse abandonment** | Product viewed 2×, no cart | +6h | Single product, single image, one line |

| 4 | **Unboxing / usage guide** | Purchase | +3 days | How to open the cap without spilling; **why throw peaks around day 2–4 as the wood saturates** (use your actual E1 data); where to hang for circulation; why not to leave it in direct summer sun; **avoid contact with painted and polished surfaces**. Measurably reduces "not strong enough" complaints |
| 5 | **Review + UGC request** | Purchase | +14 days | Judge.me request. Free refill for any video review — this is how you generate ad creative for free |
| 6 | **Refill reminder** | Purchase | +30 days | "Your vessel is roughly half empty." One-click reorder with the exact scent pre-loaded |
| 7 | **Subscription pitch** | Purchase | +38 days | "The Reserve: two refills every 60 days, £16 delivered, 15% off everything else, first access to seasonal scents." Emphasise cancel-anytime |
| 8 | **Win-back** | No order in 75 days | Day 75 | New scent announcement, not a discount |
| 9 | **Subscriber welcome** | Subscription started | Immediate | Set the next ship date with total clarity. Unclear ship dates are the #1 cancellation cause |
| 10 | **Pre-renewal notice** | 5 days before charge | Automated | Reduces chargebacks. **Include a "swap my scent this cycle" link** |

**Why flow 10's swap link matters more than it looks:** fragrance subscribers churn from boredom, not price. Free scent rotation each cycle converts a 4-month average lifespan into 12+. It is the highest-ROI retention mechanic in this category.

### 6.2 Feeding data into AI

**Weekly (15 minutes, Sundays).** Export Orders CSV, Products-Sold-by-Variant, Sessions-by-Traffic-Source, plus Judge.me reviews. Then:

> *"Attached are my Shopify order export, product-sold-by-variant report and traffic-source report for the past 7 days. Return: (1) revenue and units by scent, with share of total and week-over-week change; (2) refill attach rate to first orders; (3) AOV by traffic source; (4) which scent has the highest repeat-purchase rate; (5) any scent below 8% of units to consider discontinuing. Show your calculations. Flag any finding where sample size is too small for the conclusion to be reliable."*

That last sentence is critical. At 40 orders most apparent patterns are noise.

**Monthly review mining:**

> *"Here are 60 customer reviews. Extract: (1) the 5 most common specific complaints, ranked by frequency; (2) the exact phrases customers use to describe each scent, for ad copy; (3) any mention of longevity, leakage, staining or throw strength, quoted with the scent name; (4) words customers use that do not appear anywhere in my current product copy."*

Point 4 is the highest-value output. Customers describe your product in language you haven't thought of, and that language converts better than anything you write. **Cross-reference complaints against your Module 2C data** — if customers report exactly what your testing predicted, your protocol is calibrated; if they report something you didn't test for, add that environment.

**Inventory projection (Day 45):**

> *"Given 60 days of sales data attached, current stock of [X] units per scent, 21-day supplier lead time and a 30-day safety buffer, calculate reorder quantity per scent and the latest date I must order. Assume 15% month-over-month growth; show the calculation with and without that assumption."*

**CAC and cohort tracking.** One Google Sheet: date, channel, spend, orders, revenue, contribution. Compute blended CAC weekly. Target: blended CAC under £12 against a £24 first-order contribution. Monthly, compute 30/60/90-day repeat rates by acquisition cohort and by first-purchased scent. **Whichever scent produces the highest 60-day repeat rate becomes your seeding and paid hero, regardless of first-order volume.**

## Honest Risk Assessment

**1. Scent returns and "it's not strong enough."** The dominant complaint in this category, and partly physics rather than quality. Passive evaporation in a 2.5m³ cabin produces modest throw. Mitigate with the Discovery Set, the day-3 usage email, and an intensity gauge calibrated from your actual E3/E4 test data. Never overclaim.

**2. Paid acquisition will not work at a £26 AOV in month one.** If organic hasn't produced traction by Day 35, the answer is more organic volume and more seeding, not more ad spend. Spending the reserve out of frustration is the most common way this business dies.

**3. The product is structurally easy to copy.** Anyone can buy the same bottles and oils. Your only durable assets are brand and the subscriber list — which is why Modules 4 and 6 matter more than Module 2, even though Module 2 feels like the real work.

**4. New in v2.0: the physical failure modes are the ones that scale badly.** A scent that stains beech or clouds in cold weather will not fail on unit one; it fails on units 1–150 simultaneously, six weeks after you shipped them, after you have spent the reorder money. **Module 2C is the cheapest insurance in this entire plan.**

## Glossary of Acronyms and Terms

### Business and commercial

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **AOV** | Average Order Value — average revenue per completed transaction |
| **B2B** | Business-to-Business — a transaction between two businesses |
| **B2C** | Business-to-Consumer — a transaction between a business and an end consumer |
| **Blended CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost across all channels combined, rather than channel by channel |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost — total spend required to acquire one paying customer |
| **Churn** | The rate at which subscribers cancel, usually a monthly percentage |
| **COGS** | Cost of Goods Sold — direct material cost of producing one unit |
| **Cohort** | A group of customers who first purchased in the same period, tracked over time |
| **Contribution margin** | Revenue minus all variable costs (COGS, postage, payment fees) |
| **CPC** | Cost Per Click — average cost each time a user clicks an ad |
| **CTA** | Call to Action — a prompt directing a user to a next step |
| **CTR** | Click-Through Rate — clicks ÷ impressions, as a percentage |
| **DDP** | Delivered Duty Paid — shipping term where the supplier covers freight, insurance and import duties to your door |
| **De minimis** | Customs duty relief threshold (£135 in the UK) below which no duty is charged, though import VAT still applies |
| **DTC** | Direct-to-Consumer — a brand selling directly to end consumers, bypassing wholesale |
| **Engagement rate** | (likes + comments) ÷ followers, as a percentage |
| **Hook rate** | Percentage of viewers who watch past the 3-second mark |
| **HS code** | Harmonised System code — international customs classification number |
| **Landing page** | The page a user arrives at after clicking an ad, built for one conversion objective |
| **MOQ** | Minimum Order Quantity — smallest quantity a supplier will sell |
| **Pixel** | Tracking code placed on your site by an ad platform to build retargeting audiences |
| **POV** | Point of View — first-person short-form video format |
| **Retargeting** | Advertising shown to people who previously visited your site |
| **RFQ** | Request for Quote — formal supplier enquiry for price, lead time and terms |
| **ROAS** | Return on Ad Spend — revenue per £1 of ad spend |
| **SKU** | Stock Keeping Unit — unique identifier for a distinct product variant |
| **Sole trader** | Simplest UK business structure; registered with HMRC for Self Assessment, no Companies House filing |
| **Spark Ad** | TikTok ad format amplifying an existing organic post from a creator's own handle |
| **UGC** | User-Generated Content — content created by customers or seeded creators |
| **UX** | User Experience — how a person feels navigating your site |
| **VAT** | Value Added Tax — UK consumption tax at 20%; registration required above £90,000 turnover |

### Regulatory and compliance

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **ATPs** | Adaptations to Technical Progress — formal updates to EU CLP classification lists |
| **CLP** | Classification, Labelling and Packaging Regulation — the chemical safety law governing hazard information on labels. GB CLP applies in England, Scotland and Wales |
| **CRC / CRF** | Child-Resistant Closure / Fastening — a tamper-resistant cap mandated by CLP Annex II for certain hazardous mixtures sold to consumers |
| **ECHA** | European Chemicals Agency — administers EU CLP, PCN and REACH |
| **EUH066** | Supplemental statement: repeated exposure may cause skin dryness or cracking |
| **EUH208** | Supplemental statement: "Contains [allergen]. May produce an allergic reaction." Used where a sensitiser is below the classification threshold |
| **EUH210** | Supplemental statement: "Safety data sheet available on request." Required on consumer products |
| **GPSR** | General Product Safety Regulation — EU law requiring an EU-established Responsible Person for goods sold to EU consumers |
| **H304** | Hazard statement: "May be fatal if swallowed and enters airways" (aspiration hazard). **Triggers CRC and tactile warning requirements.** Driven by low kinematic viscosity plus hydrocarbon/terpene content |
| **H statement** | Hazard statement — standardised CLP phrase describing a hazard (H315 skin irritation, H317 allergic skin reaction, H319 eye irritation, H411 aquatic toxicity) |
| **HSE** | Health and Safety Executive — UK regulator for chemicals legislation including GB CLP |
| **IFRA** | International Fragrance Association — sets maximum safe usage limits for fragrance ingredients by product category. Car and reed diffusers typically fall in Category 11 or 12 |
| **NPIS** | National Poisons Information Service — the GB body equivalent to EU poison centres |
| **P statement** | Precautionary statement — standardised CLP phrase on safe handling and response |
| **PCN** | Poison Centre Notification — mandatory submission linking a product's UFI to its composition. Required for EU and Northern Ireland, not GB |
| **SDS** | Safety Data Sheet — standardised 16-section supplier document. §2.2 gives classification, §3 composition, §9.1 flashpoint, §11 toxicology |
| **STOT** | Specific Target Organ Toxicity — CLP classification for chemicals harming specific organs |
| **Tactile warning** | A raised triangle moulded into packaging, required alongside CRC for certain classifications, so visually impaired users can identify a hazardous product by touch |
| **TRA** | Toxicological Risk Assessment — safety evaluation of a product's ingredients |
| **UFI** | Unique Formula Identifier — 16-character code linking a product to its PCN dossier. Mandatory for EU and Northern Ireland; **voluntary for Great Britain** |

### Fragrance chemistry and testing

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Aldehydes (C10–C12)** | Light, sparkling synthetic molecules giving "fizz" to clean and laundry accords. Highly volatile — they flash off fast in a wooden-cap diffuser |
| **Ambroxan / Ambrocenide** | Synthetic ambergris molecules (MW ~236). High substantivity, moderate volatility — near-ideal for car diffusion |
| **Augeo Clean Multi** | A propanediol-based ether/ester carrier base used to dilute fragrance oil in diffusers. Low odour, biodegradable, high flash point. **Strips paint and polish on contact** |
| **Calone** | The signature marine/ozonic molecule behind most aquatic accords. Light and volatile |
| **Cashmeran (DPMI)** | Warm woody-musky-powdery molecule (MW 206). Exceptionally substantive and wicks well through wood — the single best-suited molecule for this product format |
| **Cloud point** | The temperature at which a blend turns hazy as components drop out of solution. Must sit below 0°C for a UK car product |
| **Coumarin** | The tonka bean molecule. Sweet, hay-like, substantive — but a declarable allergen and skin sensitiser |
| **Dihydromyrcenol** | The "fresh clean" molecule behind most designer blue fragrances. Light, and often a terpene-load concern |
| **Discolouration** | Browning of the oil or the wooden cap, usually caused by vanillin oxidation. The primary cosmetic failure mode for gourmand scents |
| **d-Limonene** | The dominant citrus terpene. **The main driver of H304 classification.** Treat above 10% as a red flag |
| **Flashpoint** | The lowest temperature at which a liquid gives off enough vapour to ignite. **Royal Mail prohibits liquids at or below 60.5°C closed-cup** |
| **Fractionation** | Selective separation of a blend as lighter molecules evaporate first, leaving only heavy base notes. The core reason a diffuser "changes smell" over weeks |
| **Galaxolide / Habanolide** | Polycyclic and macrocyclic musks. Very substantive; Galaxolide's size can contribute to wood glazing |
| **Glazing** | A shiny film of deposited heavy material on the wood cap that blocks the capillary path and kills throw |
| **Headspace saturation** | Accumulation of fragrance vapour in an enclosed volume. Produces the "cabin-fill hit" when a car door is opened after standing |
| **Iso E Super** | Velvety woody-amber molecule (MW 234). Transparent, extremely substantive, widely used as a diffusive backbone |
| **Kinematic viscosity** | Resistance to flow. Low viscosity combined with hydrocarbon content is what drives H304 aspiration classification |
| **MW (Molecular Weight)** | Mass of a molecule. Broadly, lower MW means faster evaporation; higher MW means slower wicking and longer persistence |
| **Negative fill** | Photography technique using black card opposite a light source to deepen shadow and add contrast |
| **Scent throw** | Strength and reach of a fragrance diffusing into surrounding air. **Static throw** = in still air; **dynamic throw** = under forced airflow |
| **Sesquiterpene** | A 15-carbon terpene class (vetiver, patchouli, guaiacwood). High MW, high substantivity, excellent fixatives |
| **Substantivity** | How long a molecule persists and continues emitting. The property that determines whether a scent survives the air conditioning |
| **Vanillin / Ethyl vanillin** | The vanilla molecules. Substantive and pleasant, but they oxidise brown, stain pale wood, and crystallise in cold |
| **Volatility** | How readily a molecule evaporates. The target band for car diffusers is *mid*-volatility: light enough to emit under airflow, heavy enough not to vanish |
